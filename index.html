Unveiling the Impact: A Story of Nepal's Earthquake

An interactive analysis of building damage, told through data.

The Data Tells a Story

Following the devastating earthquake in Nepal, a rich dataset was compiled to understand the factors influencing building damage. This data isn't just numbers; it's a window into the lives of families and communities affected. Our analysis aims to uncover the patterns and relationships hidden within this data, transforming raw information into a clear and compelling narrative.

Join us as we explore the key factors that determined whether a home would withstand the tremors or fall to rubble.

Chapter 1: The Building's Blueprint

Before we can understand the damage, we must first understand the buildings themselves. The dataset provides a detailed blueprint for each structure. The key parameters considered for this analysis are:

Age

The number of years the building has stood, a key indicator of material degradation and construction standards over time.

Floors

The number of stories in the building, which directly impacts its height and stability during seismic events.

Superstructure

The primary load-bearing materials, which can include adobe, mud mortar stone, stone flag, cement mortar brick, timber, and bamboo.

Foundation

The material and type of the building's foundation, providing crucial support and stability from the ground up.

Location

The specific geographical location of the building within Nepal, which may correlate with ground conditions and local seismic intensity.

Chapter 2: Uncovering the Insights

With a clear understanding of the parameters, let's dive into the visualizations to see how these factors influenced the damage grade. The following dashboards allow us to explore the data and find key insights.

Age and Floors vs. Damage Grade

Key Insight: As seen in the scatter plot, newer buildings and those with fewer floors generally experienced less severe damage. However, the data shows that this is not always the case, suggesting other factors, such as materials, are also at play.

Material's Resilience: A Tale of Superstructures

This dashboard classifies buildings by their superstructure and foundation types, directly showing how material choices impacted earthquake resilience.

Key Insight: Structures built with reinforced concrete (RC) demonstrated a clear advantage with significantly lower damage grades. Conversely, traditional materials like adobe and mud mortar stone were more vulnerable.

The Lay of the Land: A Geographical Heatmap

The ground a building stands on is just as important as the building itself. This heatmap visualizes the concentration of different damage grades across Nepal's geographical regions, allowing us to see how local conditions may have influenced the destruction.

Key Insight: The heatmap reveals that certain geographical regions experienced a higher prevalence of severe damage (Damage Grade 3), highlighting the impact of factors like soil type, proximity to the epicenter, or regional construction practices.

The Aftermath and the Path Forward

Our analysis paints a clear picture: building age, materials, and geographical location were all significant predictors of earthquake damage. The data not only helps us understand the past but also informs a path forward for building a more resilient Nepal. Future efforts should focus on retrofitting vulnerable structures, promoting safer construction materials, and developing building codes tailored to regional geological conditions.

By turning data into a story, we hope to make these critical insights more accessible and inspire action for a safer tomorrow.
